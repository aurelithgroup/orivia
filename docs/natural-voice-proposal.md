# Natural voice for Listen: capped pilot proposal (22 Oct, Cleo)

Status: **built and released 22 Oct** (founder approved $1/day, $5/month). See docs/connected-orivia-plan.md for the release record.

## Today

- Listen uses the phone's own voices, which is free.
- Since step 5, the voice follows the language the reply is actually written in, not a guess.
- Quality depends on the phone. Some phones have no voice for Bengali or Malayalam, for example.

## What would change

- When a person taps Listen, Orivia sends **only that reply's text** to OpenAI and plays back the voice OpenAI generates.
- It never sends the whole chat or microphone audio, and never plays anything automatically.
- The model is `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini`, through OpenAI's Realtime API. The older `tts-1` and `gpt-4o-mini-tts` shut down on 6 Jan 2027, so it isn't worth building on them.
- The phone voice stays as the fallback: when the allowance is used up, OpenAI is unavailable, or the person turns it off in Settings.
- The consent text and Settings will say that replies read aloud are AI-generated by OpenAI.
- Generated audio is never stored or shared between people.
- Before any wider release, check that the voice reads the sourced reply word for word.

## Spending cap

- The voice draws on the **existing** OpenAI limits ($5/day, $20/month); those limits are not raised.
- Inside them, voice gets its own smaller allowance: **$1/day and $5/month** to start.
- When the voice allowance runs out, Listen quietly switches back to the phone voice, so chat answers are never blocked by voice spending.
- OpenAI prices audio output at about $20 per million audio tokens. A cost per minute is only known once the pilot is measured, so the first week on the pilot is for measuring that before any number is promised.

## Languages

OpenAI lists English, French, Arabic, Hindi, Urdu, Chinese, Filipino (Tagalog) and Russian. Bengali and Malayalam aren't listed; those stay on the phone voice unless a test shows they work well.

## What the founder decides

1. Whether to build this for the pilot with the $1/day and $5/month voice allowance (or a different amount).
2. After hearing samples on your own phone: whether it sounds good enough to release.
